# Build a Houston Food Web Card

Follow this choose-your-path guide to turn a real organism into a card that fits our game. Your challenge is to show an ecological relationship using the game's existing actions.

**Your finished card needs one main action and, if useful, one special ability.** A second ability is optional. An interesting organism does not need a new rule to be interesting!

## Start: Who is your organism?

Use a reliable field guide, museum, university, or wildlife agency source. Write down its common name, scientific name, and family. Check that it lives in or visits the Houston area. Keep your source links in your class notes.

Ask these questions before writing an action:

- Is it a plant, animal, fungus, or bacterium?
- What does it eat? Does its diet change between young and adult stages?
- Which organisms already in our deck could it interact with?
- Does it pollinate a flower, rely on a host plant, provide protection, or recycle dead material?
- What real trait might justify a small advantage?

**Choose your path:**

| If your organism… | Go to |
| --- | --- |
| Is a plant that uses sunlight to grow | A: Plant |
| Is an animal that eats plants, leaves, or sap | B: Herbivore |
| Is an animal that eats animals | C: Predator |
| Visits flowers and transfers pollen | D: Pollinator |
| Is a fungus or bacterium that breaks down dead material | E: Decomposer |
| Fits more than one role | F: More than one role |
| Does not fit these paths | Ask your teacher to help choose the closest existing action |

## A: Plant

**Main action — copy this into the first action box:**

`Grow: Spend 1 Light + 1 Water for +1 Population. Repeat while able, up to the maximum.`

Plants grow during the Plant Growth phase. Printing Grow on your card does not give it another growth phase or a free turn action.

**What interacts with your plant?** Find a pollinator or herbivore in the deck. Check whether that animal's printed target includes your plant. A new plant does not automatically change another card's diet.

**Is it a special host plant?** Go to G and choose Host Plant. **Does it resist drought or support a large population?** Choose Drought Tolerant or Large Plant. **No special advantage?** Leave the second action blank or use a supported pollination relationship from G. Then go to H.

## B: Herbivore

**What does it actually eat?** Name one or a short list of compatible plants from the deck. A specialist should not be allowed to eat every plant.

**Copy this, replacing the brackets:**

`Feed: Choose [plant names] anywhere in the garden. [Your organism] +1 Population; plant -1 Population.`

For a caterpillar that needs one host plant, use:

`Caterpillar Feeding: Feed on [host plant]. [Your organism] +1 Population; plant -1 Population.`

The Monarch's caterpillar feeds on Milkweed; that does not mean every butterfly's caterpillar does. Research your species and its life stage.

**Does it harvest seeds without seriously harming the plant?** The expansion's Red Harvester Ant offers a more specific pattern:

`Gather Seeds: Use [seed-producing plant] in the same plot. [Your organism] +1 Population; the plant is unchanged.`

Use this only for a supported seed relationship and have your teacher check it: gaining Population without costing the plant anything is a stronger benefit than ordinary feeding. Otherwise use Feed. Go to G for an optional ability, then H.

## C: Predator

**Which prey really belongs in its diet?** Choose named prey from the deck. Avoid “anything” or “any animal.” A specialist's short prey list is part of its design.

**Copy this, replacing the brackets:**

`Hunt: Choose [prey names] anywhere in the garden. [Your organism] +1 Population; prey -1 Population.`

For an animal whose young develop in and kill a host, the same basic exchange can be labeled:

`Parasitize: Choose [host animal]. [Your organism] +1 Population; host -1 Population.`

Do not copy the prototype wasp's face-down card conversion as a default. It is a special mechanic, not a general rule for all parasitoids.

**Can it hunt Aphids?** If it is an insect predator, use the deck's restriction in the second box:

`Ant Protection: Cannot hunt Aphids protected by Red Imported Fire Ants.`

Anoles and Wrens have printed exceptions. Do not give a new predator that exception automatically. For spiders or other uncertain cases, ask your teacher which protection rule applies.

A restriction can fill your second box; you do not need to add a boost too. Go to H.

## D: Pollinator

**Does your animal transfer pollen while visiting these flowers?** Nectar drinking alone is not enough evidence. Identify compatible flowering plants in the deck.

**Copy this, replacing the brackets:**

`Pollinate: Visit [flowering plant names] anywhere in the garden. [Your organism] and plant each gain +1 Population.`

Use “any flowering plant” only when your research supports a broad diet, as on the Carpenter Bee card. Both partners keep their own Population maximum.

**Do the young have a different diet?** A butterfly can use Pollinate as its first action and Caterpillar Feeding from B as its second. Those are choices: activating it lets you choose one action, not do both. Go to H.

## E: Decomposer

**Does it break down dead material?** Fungi and bacteria can use this path. An animal that eats microbes or dead material is not automatically a fungus-style decomposer; ask your teacher which existing pattern fits. The expansion's Springtails, for example, feed on live decomposers.

**Copy the normal decomposer action:**

`Decompose: Turn 1 face-up organism in Compost face down on bottom. Give one of your plants +1 Population.`

You must have an eligible face-up organism card in Compost and a plant with room to grow. The decomposer does not gain Population from this action. Face-down cards and Effect cards are not food for this action.

**Optional second box — the existing play rule:**

`Always Playable: A decomposer may enter your plot even when no food or prey is present.`

This reminds players of a rule; it is not an extra boost. Keep faster recycling or self-growth ideas for teacher review. Go to H.

## F: More than one role

Choose the organism's most important relationship for the first box. A second supported relationship may fill the second box instead of a special ability.

- Butterfly: Pollinate + Caterpillar Feeding.
- Omnivore: Feed + Hunt, each with a researched target list.
- Predator with a protection restriction: Hunt + Ant Protection.

**One activation means one chosen action.** Two printed actions do not mean two activations. A passive ability applies only when its printed condition happens. Do not add a third action to the fact box. Go to H, or G if you still have an empty second box.

## G: Choose one optional special ability

Choose an ability only if research supports it. You may leave the second box blank. Each example below reuses a pattern already printed in the deck; your teacher still checks the targets and balance before adding the card.

### Does another organism depend on this host plant?

For a researched caterpillar–plant relationship, copy:

`Host Plant: [caterpillar name] feeds on this plant. [Animal name] +1 Population; this plant -1 Population.`

This describes the animal's Feed action; it does not let the plant activate to feed an animal or grant a second feeding reward. Use “feeds only on” only when your source supports that restriction. Both partner cards should agree about the target. If the host's partner is absent, plan the pair with your teacher.

### Does your plant tolerate drought?

Copy the Frogfruit pattern:

`Drought Tolerant: Not affected by drought cards.`

This protects it from the Drought card. It does not provide Water, allow water-free growth, or automatically protect it from Heat Wave. Choose it because of evidence about the plant, not simply because it grows in Texas.

### Does it form a large stand or colony?

For a plant, copy:

`Large Plant: [Plant name] may hold up to 4 Population instead of the usual 3.`

For a colony-forming animal, copy:

`Large Colony: [Animal name] may hold up to 4 Population instead of the usual 3.`

These follow Sunflower and Texas Leafcutting Ant. A larger maximum is room to grow, not free Population: the organism still enters with 1. It does not raise the 3-Water cap. Do not increase the maximum beyond 4 or give every abundant species this ability.

### Does your flowering plant support pollinators?

Copy, with a supported pollinator or group:

`Pollination: [pollinator names] may visit. Both pollinator and plant gain +1 Population.`

This states a compatible pollination relationship. It gives the usual benefit once when the pollinator acts, not another bonus on top of Pollinate.

### Does it have an ant–aphid honeydew relationship?

For Aphids paired with the existing Fire Ant card, the deck uses:

`Honeydew Mutualism: When these Aphids feed successfully, Fire Ants in the same plot also gain +1 Population.`

That is one shared benefit described on both partner cards, not two separate rewards. A different ant–aphid pair needs teacher review and matching text on both cards; do not assume all ants protect all aphids. Protection changes which predators can hunt the aphids, so it affects other cards too.

### Is your “superpower” something else?

First try one of these translations:

| Your idea | A way to fit this game |
| --- | --- |
| “It reproduces quickly!” | Consider maximum 4 Population if justified; keep ordinary action gains at +1. |
| “It has a very specialized diet!” | Name its few valid food or prey cards. A limitation can be interesting. |
| “It is active at night!” | Put the fact in Interesting fact; do not add night turns. |
| “It migrates, has venom, or is camouflaged!” | Explain the ecology in Interesting fact; keep the ordinary action. |
| “It farms a fungus!” | Explain the relationship in Interesting fact; the Leafcutting Ant already uses leaf harvesting with normal +1/-1 feeding. |
| “It needs a new resource, extra turn, or automatic attack!” | Save the idea in class notes for a future game version; use an existing action for this deck. |

## H: Write the card

In the Houston Food Web editor, choose **New card**. Complete the name, scientific name, family, and category fields. Use a category such as plant · flowering, herbivore, predator, pollinator · herbivore, or decomposer · fungus.

Put your main action in the first action box and your optional ability or second action in the second. Replace every bracket with an actual name. Use the deck's printed Common Names so players know exactly which card you mean.

Put one short, researched ecological fact in Interesting fact. This is a good home for an exciting trait that does not need a rule. Add your own drawing or suitable artwork. If text does not fit, shorten the target list or wording while keeping the condition, target, and Population change clear. Do not hide rules in the fact box.

### Worked example: a lady beetle

The expansion already provides a model for adding another aphid predator:

- **Common name:** Convergent Lady Beetle
- **Scientific name:** Hippodamia convergens
- **Family:** Coccinellidae
- **Category:** native predator · insect
- **First action:** Hunt Aphids: Take Aphids anywhere. Lady Beetle +1 Population; Aphids -1 Population.
- **Second ability:** Ant Protection: Cannot hunt Aphids protected by Red Imported Fire Ants.
- **Interesting fact:** Adults and larvae both eat aphids.

It adds a real food-web connection with the usual Hunt exchange. It does not need extra turns or a new counter.

## Finish: Check your card with a partner

1. **Evidence:** Can you show a source for its diet, partner, and special trait?
2. **Connection:** Is at least one usable partner already in the deck? Can an existing predator eat your new animal, where appropriate? Check both cards' target text; ask your teacher before changing an existing card.
3. **Action:** Does your text say who is targeted, what happens, and how much changes? Ordinary Feed and Hunt give +1 to the eater and -1 to the food; Pollinate gives +1 to each partner.
4. **Limits:** It enters with 1 Population and normally has maximum 3. Every gain respects the maximum. Water stays capped at 3 per plant. No new counters, dice, extra turns, unlimited growth, or automatic wins.
5. **Timing:** Plants grow in Plant Growth. On your turn you activate one organism you control; a second printed action is an alternative, not an extra activation. Targets may be anywhere unless your card says “same plot.”
6. **Compost:** Lost counters return to the supply. An organism losing its last Population goes face up to Compost. Do not remove whole prey cards with an ordinary Hunt.
7. **Readability:** All brackets are replaced, names match the deck, and the editor reports that the text fits.
8. **Play test:** Try it with no partner, with a valid partner, and at its Population maximum. Let your teacher resolve any uncertain timing or full-cap interaction. Check that a special ability has not paid the same reward twice.

**Teacher check, then Save project.** Save an editable project ZIP before printing or sharing. Your teacher can combine approved cards into the class deck. Adding a card to your browser workspace does not automatically add it to everyone else's deck. Start with a teacher-chosen copy count and play-test it before increasing copies.

## If the prototype disagrees with itself

Use the [Game rules](campus-food-web-turn-guide.md) and printed cards with your teacher. The prototype has a few unresolved differences: the turn guide describes Soil Bacterium's Rapid Recycling while its current card prints Multiply; Mulch mentions hot-day evaporation that the weather table does not specify; and the wasp has a special card-conversion action. This guide does not settle those differences or use them as default new-card patterns. Your teacher should choose the ruling before play.
