# Campus Food Web — Prototype Turn Guide

## Game at a Glance

**Players:** 2–4  
**Deck:** 40 organism cards + 8 effect cards  
**Goal:** First player to reach 20 points at the end of their turn.

Each player controls a personal **Garden Plot**, but all plots form one connected ecosystem. Organisms may pollinate, feed, hunt, or parasitize across the whole table unless a card says otherwise.

## Counters and Score

Use **black-eyed peas** as counters if available. Set aside two peas marked on one side for the weather toss. The remaining, unmarked peas form the shared counter supply. Counter location tells you what it represents:

| Counter location | Meaning |
|---|---|
| On an organism card | Population |
| Above a plant card | Light |
| Below a plant card | Water |

Every organism enters play with **1 Population**. The usual maximum is **3 Population**; Common Sunflower may have 4. A plant may hold **at most 3 Water**, even if an effect would give it more. Return excess or spent counters to the supply. Light does not carry into the next round.

At the **end of your turn**, count:

1. Every Population counter on organisms in your plot; plus
2. One point for each **different organism type** in your plot (compare printed Common Names). Multiple copies of the same organism add only one diversity point.

Water, Light, cards in your hand, Effect cards, and cards in Compost do not score. Scores are not cumulative. The first player whose current plot reaches **20 or more** points at this check wins.

## Setup

1. Shuffle the organism and Effect cards together into one draw deck. Each player draws until they have 4 cards in hand.
2. Create a shared counter supply and an empty shared **Compost pile**. There is no separate discard pile and no Nutrient counter type.
3. Mark one side of each of two black-eyed peas. Put these two **weather peas** aside; they never enter the counter supply.
4. Choose a starting player.

## Round Guide

### Weather

Toss the two weather peas once for the whole garden. **Heads** means marked side up; **tails** means unmarked side up.

| Toss | Weather | Light above each plant | Stored Water |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Both heads | **Hot day** | 2 | Each plant loses 1 Water, if it has any; Frogfruit and a plant protected by Mulch ignore this evaporation. |
| Both tails | **Clear day** | 1 | No evaporation. |
| One of each | **Cloudy day** | 0 | No evaporation. |

The toss gives a 1-in-4 chance of Hot, 1-in-4 chance of Clear, and 1-in-2 chance of Cloudy. Return lost Water to the supply. Frogfruit and Mulch prevent only **Hot-day weather evaporation**; Drought and Heat Wave Effect cards still remove their Water.

### Water

Each player takes **3 counters** from the supply and distributes them as Water below plants in their own plot. No plant may hold more than **3 Water**. Return any counters that cannot be placed to the supply. Water not spent this round may stay under a plant, up to that cap.

### Plant Growth

Each plant may repeatedly spend **1 Light + 1 Water** to gain **+1 Population** per pair spent. It can grow more than once in the same phase if it has enough of both resources, but never above its Population maximum. Return spent Light and Water to the supply. Unused Light is cleared at Cleanup.

### Player Turns

Beginning with the starting player, each player takes one turn:

1. **Draw 1 card.**
2. **Play up to 1 card.** This may be an organism or an Effect card.
3. **Activate 1 organism you control.** A newly played organism may activate immediately. You may skip this step.
4. **Check your score.** First to 20 or more wins.

Turn an activated organism sideways. Each organism can activate only once per round. Card-specific rules override the shared action only where they say so.

### Cleanup

Return all unused Light to the supply. Straighten activated cards. Water remains below plants, up to 3 per plant. Pass the starting-player marker clockwise and begin the next round.

## Ecosystem Actions

### Pollinate — Mutualism

Choose a compatible flowering plant anywhere in the garden. The pollinator and plant each gain **+1 Population**, subject to their individual maxima.

### Feed — Herbivory

Choose a compatible plant anywhere in the garden. The herbivore gains **+1 Population** and the plant loses **1 Population**, subject to the herbivore's maximum.

### Hunt / Parasitize — Predation

Choose compatible prey anywhere in the garden. The predator gains **+1 Population** and the prey loses **1 Population**, subject to the predator's maximum.

### Decompose

A Bacterium or Fungus you control may choose **one face-up organism card** anywhere in the shared Compost pile. Turn that card face down and move it to the **bottom** of Compost. Give **one of your plants +1 Population**, up to its maximum. You need both a face-up card and a plant with room to grow. The decomposer does **not** gain Population from this action. Soil Bacterium's Rapid Recycling ability can consume two eligible cards instead, as printed on its card. Each consumed card gives +1 Population to a plant; the two gains may go to the same plant if it has room for both, or to different plants. If there are not two usable cards and two available growth spaces, use the normal one-card action.

## Compost and Discarding

Population counters lost for any reason go back to the **supply**, not to Compost. When an organism loses its last Population, return its other counters to the supply and place its **card face up on top of Compost**. An organism card discarded from your hand also goes face up on top. These face-up cards can be decomposed once each.

After a card is decomposed, it stays **face down on the bottom of Compost** and cannot be decomposed again. A played Effect card also goes **face down on the bottom of Compost**, where it is never decomposable. The exception is **Mulch**: keep it under the chosen plant until the start of your next turn, then place it face down on the bottom. When adding a face-down card, slide it under all face-up cards so available material remains visible.

## Playing Cards

Plants and decomposers can always be played. An animal can be played only if at least one organism it can use for food, prey, or pollination already exists somewhere in the shared garden. Newly played organisms enter with 1 Population.

Play an Effect card during the **“Play up to 1 card”** step instead of an organism. Resolve it immediately unless its card says it remains in play. **Compost Addition** requires an organism card in your hand to discard. For this prototype, **Native Plant Boom may target any of the five Plant card types**; native eligibility is a game rule for this demo, not an extra field on the cards. Effect cards never count as organisms or score diversity points.

## Prototype Notes

- Pollination is intentionally powerful because both partners benefit, but Population maxima still apply.
- The Aphid–Fire Ant relationship demonstrates a second kind of mutualism: aphids provide honeydew while ants protect them from some natural enemies.
- Compost is now a **card pile**, not a pile of counters. It fills when cards leave play or when Compost Addition discards a card from hand.
- After the next play test, note whether the 1-in-2 Cloudy result slows the race to 20, whether face-up Compost is available often enough for decomposers, and whether Rapid Recycling is too strong when several organisms have been discarded.
